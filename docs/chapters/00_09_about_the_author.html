00.09. About the Author

13 min estimated reading · Unit 9 of 57

On this page

This unit identifies Hao Hoang, explains his public writing practice and sole authorship, records why he wrote the book, and preserves the stated contact and correction routes.

TL;DR

  • Hao Hoang is an applied artificial intelligence and machine learning (AI/ML) engineer and technical writer.
  • He is based in Los Angeles, California, and is originally from Quang Tri, Viet Nam.
  • He owns AI Interview Prep and writes for the community every day on Substack and LinkedIn.
  • His Substack includes Daily AI Interview Questions and longer notes on large language model (LLM) system design, reinforcement learning, vision-language models, and retrieval-augmented generation. It also covers other high-signal topics for engineers and researchers preparing for rigorous AI interviews.
  • His shorter daily LinkedIn posts carry the same thread of ideas between Substack editions. His public writing reaches more than 70,000 LinkedIn followers and 16,000 Substack readers.
  • He describes that public writing as the main place where he teaches, learns in public, and answers community questions.
  • He is the sole author. He researched and wrote every chapter himself, without co-authors or ghostwriters. He wrote the book because Retrieval-Augmented Generation (RAG) is the pattern he sees deployed most often and understood least well. The questions that distinguish a competent RAG answer from a superficial one were scattered across papers, courses, and production postmortems.

The story

Picture a public workshop run by Hao Hoang, an applied AI/ML engineer and technical writer. The workshop is based in Los Angeles, while a plaque records his origin in Quang Tri, Viet Nam. AI Interview Prep is the name above the door. One workbench is Substack, where he lays out Daily AI Interview Questions and longer technical notes. A second bench is LinkedIn, where the same ideas appear as shorter daily posts between editions.

The workshop is busy. More than 70,000 LinkedIn followers and 16,000 Substack readers can follow the work. Hao uses it to teach, learn in public, and answer what the community is asking about. The book is a manual made at the central bench by one craftsperson. He researched and wrote every chapter without co-authors or ghostwriters. He built it because retrieval-augmented generation is the pattern he sees deployed most often and understood least well. The questions that separate a competent RAG answer from a superficial one were stored in separate bins across papers, courses, and production postmortems. Email is the inspection desk. Readers can report arithmetic mistakes there so the next edition improves through the same habit the book promotes: derive numbers and check them.
