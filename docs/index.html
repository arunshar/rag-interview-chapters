Front matter

  • 00.01. Title Page8 minYou can identify the edition, its scope and provenance, and the basic RAG terminology.
  • 00.02. Contents26 minYou can locate each topic in the original book through its complete section and printed-page map.
  • 00.03. Preface27 minYou can explain why RAG design requires reasoning about production decisions and their consequences.
  • 00.04. How to Use This Book27 minYou can choose a reading route, follow its prerequisites, and use the recurring study sections.
  • 00.05. For Interview Candidates35 minYou can prepare for design, derivation, and debugging questions with deliberate interview practice.
  • 00.06. For Practicing Engineers27 minYou can connect the material to engineering decisions, failure modes, and operational costs.
  • 00.07. Notation and Symbols18 minYou can interpret the symbols, abbreviations, and measurement conventions used across the book.
  • 00.08. Acknowledgments15 minYou can identify the contributions and sources that shaped the book and its treatment of negative results.
  • 00.09. About the Author13 minYou can identify Hao Hoang as the source author and locate the stated contact and correction channels.

Part I. The RAG Interview Landscape

  • Chapter 1. What a RAG Interview Actually Tests65 minYou can choose where knowledge belongs, price that choice, and recognize when retrieval is the wrong tool.
  • Chapter 2. The Competing Answers to the Same Problem70 minYou can compare RAG with continual learning, model editing, long context, and generative retrieval under stated constraints.
  • Chapter 3. A Repeatable Framework for Any RAG Design Question64 minYou can turn a design prompt into requirements, sizing estimates, component decisions, and measurable trade-offs.

Part II. The Generator Side

  • Chapter 4. What the LLM Brings and What It Breaks61 minYou can distinguish generator capabilities from factual failures and assign each failure to the component responsible for fixing it.
  • Chapter 5. Data, Privacy, and the Legal Surface60 minYou can explain how RAG changes data risks, evidence, and reversibility without claiming that architecture settles legal questions.
  • Chapter 6. Scaling Laws and the Economics of Retrieval vs Parameters47 minYou can compare retrieval and model capacity through training, serving, memory, and compute budgets.
  • Chapter 7. In-Context Learning - The Mechanism RAG Rides On53 minYou can explain how in-context learning supports RAG and when supervised fine-tuning better serves the task.
  • Chapter 8. Reading the Machine: Circuits, Induction Heads, and Attribution47 minYou can use circuits, induction heads, and attribution to reason about failures while respecting the limits of interpretability evidence.

Part III. Prompting and Context Construction

  • Chapter 9. Prompting for Retrieval-Augmented Systems43 minYou can choose prompting and reasoning scaffolds while accounting for the costs and failures of extra calls or passages.
  • Chapter 10. Prompt Sensitivity53 minYou can measure changes caused by equivalent prompt wording before selecting a template.
  • Chapter 11. Abstention and Calibration50 minYou can design abstention rules and assess confidence calibration for short and long answers.

Part IV. Representing What You Retrieve

  • Chapter 12. Text Representation55 minYou can compare text representations through exact matching, semantic reach, training cost, and operational stability.
  • Chapter 13. Chunking and Granularity60 minYou can choose retrieval granularity, preserve context, and test whether a chunking method improves retrieval.
  • Chapter 14. Beyond Plain Text - Tables, Layout, Documents57 minYou can preserve table and document structure and explain the assumptions and costs of layout-aware representations.

Part V. Indexing and Vector Search

  • Chapter 15. Approximate Nearest Neighbor Search66 minYou can choose an approximate nearest neighbor index using recall, latency, memory, build time, and update constraints.
  • Chapter 16. Compression and Index Economics37 minYou can calculate index memory and compare compression methods without confusing vector storage with the full system footprint.
  • Chapter 17. Operating a Vector Store66 minYou can plan vector-store deletion, growth, sharding, metadata filters, and query constraints.

Part VI. Retrieval and Ranking

  • Chapter 18. Classical IR You Are Expected to Know55 minYou can derive lexical ranking behavior and explain why sparse retrieval remains valuable for rare and exact-match queries.
  • Chapter 19. Learning to Rank58 minYou can connect ranking objectives to metrics and compare pointwise, pairwise, listwise, and neural ranking designs.
  • Chapter 20. Dense Retrieval60 minYou can train and serve dense retrieval, choose negatives, and combine semantic search with lexical search.
  • Chapter 21. Learned Sparse and Multi-Vector Retrieval62 minYou can compare learned sparse and multi-vector retrieval and price hybrid fusion, compression, and pruning.
  • Chapter 22. Reranking50 minYou can choose a reranker, size its candidate set, and separate retrieval failures from model failures.
  • Chapter 23. Generative Retrieval57 minYou can explain retrieval through generated document identifiers and its limits for quality and index updates.

Part VII. Query Understanding and Control Flow

  • Chapter 24. Query Reformulation59 minYou can diagnose query failures and choose expansion, rewriting, conversational repair, or hypothetical-answer retrieval.
  • Chapter 25. Routing and Adaptive Retrieval67 minYou can decide when and where to retrieve and test how learned routing behaves on unfamiliar queries.
  • Chapter 26. Iterative, Recursive, and Agentic Retrieval51 minYou can compare repeated retrieval loops and set stopping rules that account for accuracy, latency, and context growth.

Part VIII. Training the RAG System

  • Chapter 27. Fine-tuning the Generator51 minYou can decide when to fine-tune the generator and match training objectives to the retrieval defects it must handle.
  • Chapter 28. Training the Retriever46 minYou can connect generator utility to retriever training and account for index refresh costs.
  • Chapter 29. Bootstrapping Training Data48 minYou can build retrieval supervision from generated data while checking relevance, distribution shift, and the teacher ceiling.

Part IX. Generation and Context Assembly

  • Chapter 30. Lost in the Middle50 minYou can diagnose position-dependent context failures and compare changes to ordering, truncation, training, and architecture.
  • Chapter 31. Attribution and Citation47 minYou can distinguish attribution from citation appearance and evaluate whether evidence supports each claim.

Part X. Evaluation

  • Chapter 32. Evaluating Retrieval39 minYou can derive retrieval metrics and assess evaluation based on relevance labels, human judgments, or LLM judges.
  • Chapter 33. Evaluating Generation45 minYou can assess generation through faithfulness, factuality, claim coverage, and response-length effects.
  • Chapter 34. Evaluating the System49 minYou can locate failures by stage and design ablations, sanity checks, and system-level evaluation.

Part XI. Trust, Credibility, and Adversarial Robustness

  • Chapter 35. Source Credibility50 minYou can evaluate source credibility while considering freshness, conflicting evidence, pluralism, and fair exposure.
  • Chapter 36. Provenance and Adversarial Robustness56 minYou can separate attribution from authenticity and address provenance, prompt injection, poisoning, and access boundaries.

Part XII. Scaling, Advanced Variants, and Design Drills

  • Chapter 37. Latency, Cost, and Systems43 minYou can build stage-level latency and cost budgets and evaluate caching, pipelining, and retrieval cascades.
  • Chapter 38. Distributed and Federated RAG43 minYou can design retrieval across independently governed sources while accounting for tenancy, residency, and shared infrastructure.
  • Chapter 39. Multimodal RAG53 minYou can compare multimodal encoding, retrieval, fusion, attribution, and evaluation across text, images, audio, and video.
  • Chapter 40. Graph RAG43 minYou can decide when a graph earns its cost and compare graph construction, retrieval, generation, and failure modes.
  • Chapter 41. End-to-End Design Drills44 minYou can answer seven design prompts with explicit constraints, measurable architectures, and defensible trade-offs.

Appendices

  • Appendix A1. Formula Sheet30 minYou can rehearse formulas for lexical scoring, rank fusion, ranking metrics, modularity, quantization, compute, and memory.
  • Appendix A2. Question Bank75 minYou can test mechanism knowledge, derivation, and judgment with Core, Senior, and Staff questions and separate answer spines.
  • Appendix A3. Design Checklists43 minYou can review a design using retrieval, capacity, evaluation, credibility, and latency checklists.
  • Appendix A4. The RAG Card and Index Datasheet32 minYou can document system behavior and index properties using the RAG Card and Index Datasheet templates.
  • Appendix A5. Annotated Reading List58 minYou can find chapter-linked readings and identify the result each reading supports.
  • Appendix A6. Notation Quick Reference14 minYou can look up symbols and calculation conventions while working through derivations.
  • Appendix A7. Glossary31 minYou can reconcile overlapping RAG terms and recognize when different names refer to the same idea.
