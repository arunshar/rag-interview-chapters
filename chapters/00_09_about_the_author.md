## The story

Picture a public
workshop run by Hao
Hoang, an applied
AI/ML engineer and
technical writer.
The workshop is
based in Los
Angeles, while a
plaque records his
origin in Quang Tri,
Viet Nam. AI
Interview Prep is
the name above the
door. One workbench
is Substack, where
he lays out Daily AI
Interview Questions
and longer technical
notes. A second
bench is LinkedIn,
where the same ideas
appear as shorter
daily posts between
editions.

The workshop is
busy. More than
70,000 LinkedIn
followers and 16,000
Substack readers can
follow the work. Hao
uses it to teach,
learn in public, and
answer what the
community is asking
about. The book is a
manual made at the
central bench by one
craftsperson. He
researched and wrote
every chapter
without co-authors
or ghostwriters. He
built it because
retrieval-augmented
generation is the
pattern he sees
deployed most often
and understood least
well. The questions
that separate a
competent RAG answer
from a superficial
one were stored in
separate bins across
papers, courses, and
production
postmortems. Email
is the inspection
desk. Readers can
report arithmetic
mistakes there so
the next edition
improves through the
same habit the book
promotes: derive
numbers and check
them.
